# Merchant Interview Guide

> 20 minutes. Once a week, and once at the end.
>
> **You are not selling. You are not explaining. You are not defending.** Every minute you spend
> talking is a minute you learn nothing.

---

## Before you start

- **Sit beside them, not opposite.** Different posture, different conversation.
- **Ask permission to write things down**, and write in their words.
- **Say this first:** _"I want to hear what is wrong with it. You cannot hurt my feelings, and
  polite answers do not help me."_
- **Never demonstrate anything during an interview.** If they ask how something works, say
  _"show me what you'd try"_ and note what they do.

## The questions

Ask them in this order. Ask **one at a time**, then stop talking.

1. **What confused you?**
2. **What was easiest?**
3. **What took the longest?**
4. **Did anything surprise you?**
5. **Did you ever worry you might lose your data?**
6. **Would you trust this instead of your notebook?**
7. **What would stop you using this every day?**

Then, only if they have not already covered it:

8. **Show me the last entry you made.** _(Watch, do not help.)_
9. **When did you last reach for your notebook?** _(And what for?)_

## After each answer, exactly one follow-up

- _"Tell me more."_
- _"When did that happen?"_
- _"What did you do then?"_
- _"Show me."_

Then silence. **Count to five before speaking.** Most of the useful answer arrives in the pause
after they think they have finished.

## Never ask

| Don't                              | Because                         | Ask instead                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| "Do you like it?"                  | They will say yes               | "What would stop you using it?"                    |
| "Was it easy?"                     | Leading                         | "What took the longest?"                           |
| "Is it faster than your notebook?" | Leading, and they will be kind  | "When did you last use your notebook?"             |
| "Would you pay for it?"            | Hypothetical; unreliable        | "What would you stop doing to make room for this?" |
| "Do you want feature X?"           | Everyone says yes to everything | "What did you do when you needed that?"            |
| "This is because…"                 | You are now defending           | _(say nothing)_                                    |

## When they report a problem

1. **Do not fix it in front of them.** Watch what they do instead.
2. **Do not explain why it happens.**
3. Ask: _"what did you expect to happen?"_
4. Write it into `BugReport.md` the same day, with their exact words.

If they believe data was lost: **stop the interview**, export their ledger immediately, then
continue.

## Closing interview — three extra questions

- **Are you going to keep using it?** _(Then be quiet. This is the answer the pilot was for.)_
- **What would have had to be different?**
- **If I took it away tomorrow, what would you miss?** _(If "nothing", that is the finding.)_

## Writing it up

Same day, in their words, before you form an opinion. Separate three things and never blur them:

- **What they did** — observed
- **What they said** — quoted
- **What you concluded** — clearly marked as yours

A merchant who says "very good" and has recorded nothing since day 3 has told you something
specific. Record both facts and let the contradiction stand.
